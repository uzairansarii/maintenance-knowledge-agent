# DOC-01 Pre-Trip Inspection Checklist

Document ID: DOC-01 | Version 1.0 | Owner: Fleet Maintenance | Applies to: tractor units, straight trucks, light vans

> Training document written for a portfolio project. Figures are illustrative and not official guidance.

## 1. Purpose and scope

Every driver completes a pre-trip inspection before moving a vehicle at the start of each shift. The inspection takes about 10 to 15 minutes and is recorded on the pre-trip log. Mechanics use the same checklist after any repair before returning a vehicle to service.

## 2. Walk-around checks

1. Check that all lights and reflectors are clean, intact and working: headlights, brake lights, turn signals, hazard lights and marker lights.
2. Look under the vehicle for fresh drips or puddles. Any leak is a defect (see DOC-05 for hydraulic fluid and DOC-09 for coolant).
3. Check tires for cuts, bulges and low pressure (see DOC-04).
4. Check wheels for missing or loose wheel nuts and rust streaks around the nuts.
5. Check mirrors, windshield and wipers for damage.

## 3. Engine compartment checks

1. Engine oil level is between the MIN and MAX marks on the dipstick.
2. Coolant level is correct in the surge tank. Only check when the engine is cold (see DOC-09).
3. Washer fluid is full.
4. Drive belts are not cracked, frayed or loose.
5. Battery terminals are tight and free from corrosion (see DOC-06).

## 4. Cab checks

1. Seat belt, horn, gauges and warning lights work. No warning lights stay on after the engine starts.
2. Steering has no unusual play.
3. Parking brake holds when applied.
4. Brake pedal feels firm and does not sink slowly to the floor.

## 5. Air brake test

1. Start the engine and run it at fast idle. Air pressure must build from 85 psi to 100 psi within 45 seconds.
2. With the engine off, fully apply the service brake and hold for one minute. Note any pressure drop (see DOC-02, section 5, for the allowed leak-down rate).
3. Pump the brake pedal until the low-air warning buzzer or light comes on. It must come on before pressure falls below 60 psi.
4. Drain air tanks daily if the vehicle does not have an automatic drain.

## 6. Completion and sign-off

Record the inspection on the pre-trip log. Report every defect using the Defect Reporting Procedure (DOC-07). If you find a Category 1 (critical) defect, do not move the vehicle. Tell your supervisor.
