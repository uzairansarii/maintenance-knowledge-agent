# DOC-04 Tire Pressure and Tread Checks

Document ID: DOC-04 | Version 1.0 | Owner: Fleet Maintenance | Applies to: all company vehicles

> Training document written for a portfolio project. Figures are illustrative and not official guidance.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This document covers how to check tire pressure, tread depth and damage. Tires are checked at every pre-trip inspection (see DOC-01) and in detail at every service.

## 2. Pressure checks

1. Check pressure when tires are cold, meaning the vehicle has been parked for at least 3 hours or driven less than 2 km.
2. Use a calibrated gauge. Do not judge pressure by looking or by kicking the tire.
3. The correct pressure is on the tire placard on the vehicle. If the placard is missing or unreadable, report it as a defect under DOC-07. Do not guess a pressure.
4. On dual wheels, the two tires must be within 5 psi of each other.
5. Pressure falls in cold weather, so check more often in winter (see DOC-08).

## 3. Tread depth limits

1. Steer axle tires: replace at 3 mm tread depth or less (about 4/32 inch).
2. Drive and trailer axle tires: replace at 1.6 mm tread depth or less (about 2/32 inch).
3. Measure in the main grooves with a tread depth gauge, at three points across the tire.

## 4. Damage checks

1. Look for cuts, bulges, exposed cord, sidewall cracks and uneven wear.
2. Check between dual tires for stones or debris.
3. Exposed cord, a bulge, or a sidewall cut that reaches the cords is a Category 1 defect. Do not move the vehicle.

## 5. Wheel nuts

Check wheel nuts for looseness and for rust trails or shiny marks around them. Loose-nut indicators, if fitted, should still point in line. Report any loose or missing nut as a Category 1 defect.

## 6. Flat or very low tires

1. If a tire is flat or has lost more than 20 percent of its target pressure, do not inflate it. A tire that has run flat can fail violently.
2. Move away from the tire, keep others clear, and call your supervisor. Re-inflation is done only by a trained person using a safety cage and a clip-on chuck with a remote hose.

## 7. Tire changes

Wheel removal follows the lift and jack rules in DOC-10. Wheel nut tightening values come from the vehicle maker's manual.
