# DOC-05 Hydraulic Leak Response

Document ID: DOC-05 | Version 1.0 | Owner: Fleet Maintenance | Applies to: trucks, trailers with hydraulic systems, lift gates, yard equipment

> Training document written for a portfolio project. Figures are illustrative and not official guidance.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This document explains what to do when you find or suspect a hydraulic fluid leak. Hydraulic systems run at very high pressure and a small leak can cause serious injury.

## 2. Hazards

1. Fluid escaping from a pinhole can be forced through skin. Never check for a leak with your hand, fingers or a rag held in your hand.
2. Hot fluid causes burns, and spilled fluid creates slip and fire hazards.
3. Use a piece of cardboard or wood to find a suspected leak, and wear safety glasses and gloves.

## 3. Immediate response

1. Stop work and stop the machine or vehicle in a safe place.
2. Lower any raised lift gate, bucket, ramp or load to the ground or onto supports.
3. Shut off the engine and remove the key.
4. Wait for the system to cool and let pressure bleed off before you go near the leak.
5. Keep other people away from the area.

## 4. Classify the leak

1. Class A, weep or seep: damp or wet fittings with no drip. Record it and report it at the end of the shift. Check the fluid level.
2. Class B, drip: fluid drips from the component. Report it, and do not use the equipment again until a mechanic has inspected it.
3. Class C, running or spraying: fluid runs, streams or sprays. Do not operate the equipment. Tag it out, use the spill kit, and call your supervisor straight away.

## 5. Containment and clean-up

1. Put absorbent pads or granules around and under the leak to stop it spreading.
2. Block drains and keep fluid away from storm drains and waterways.
3. Place used absorbent in the oily-waste bin (see DOC-03, section 5).
4. Tell your supervisor about any spill larger than 5 litres so it can be reported to the environmental officer.

## 6. Injection injury first aid

If fluid has penetrated the skin, even if the wound looks small or painless, treat it as a medical emergency. Call emergency services or go to hospital at once. Tell the doctor that it was high-pressure hydraulic fluid. Do not wipe, squeeze or try to treat the wound yourself.

## 7. Reporting

Every hydraulic leak is reported under the Defect Reporting Procedure (DOC-07). Class C leaks are Category 1 defects. Confirm the return-to-service plan with a supervisor.
