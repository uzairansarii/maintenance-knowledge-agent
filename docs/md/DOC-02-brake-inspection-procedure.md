# DOC-02 Brake Inspection Procedure

Document ID: DOC-02 | Version 1.0 | Owner: Fleet Maintenance | Applies to: tractor units and trailers with air brakes

> Training document written for a portfolio project. Figures are illustrative and not official guidance.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This procedure sets how often brakes are inspected and what the mechanic checks. Brakes are safety-critical. If you are unsure about any step, stop and ask your supervisor.

## 2. Inspection interval

1. Tractor units: a full brake inspection every 6 weeks or every 10,000 km, whichever comes first.
2. Trailers: a full brake inspection every 8 weeks.
3. Any vehicle that has been in a collision, or reported a brake defect, gets an inspection before returning to service, whatever the schedule says.
4. Drivers also check brakes at every pre-trip inspection (see DOC-01, section 5).

## 3. Lining and pad wear limits

1. Disc brake pads: replace when friction material is less than 3 mm thick.
2. Drum brake shoe linings: replace when friction material is less than 6 mm thick.
3. Replace brake parts in axle sets (both wheel ends on the same axle), never one side only.

## 4. Drums and rotors

1. Look for cracks, deep scoring and blue heat marks. A cracked drum or rotor is a Category 1 defect.
2. Check for oil or grease contamination on linings. Contaminated linings are replaced and the leak source found.
3. Lift the vehicle only following the lift and jack rules in DOC-10 before removing wheels.

## 5. Air system leak-down test

1. Build the system to full pressure, then shut off the engine and release the parking brake.
2. Wait one minute for pressure to settle, then fully apply the service brake.
3. Allowed pressure drop with the brake applied: no more than 2 psi per minute for a single vehicle, and no more than 3 psi per minute for a tractor and trailer combination.
4. A leak-down result above the limit is a Category 1 defect. Do not release the vehicle.

## 6. Slack adjusters and pushrod stroke

1. Measure pushrod stroke with the brakes applied and compare to the limit marked on the brake chamber.
2. Automatic slack adjusters should not need manual adjustment. If one is out of adjustment, treat that as a fault in the adjuster or its mounting. Do not just adjust it and move on.

## 7. Safety-critical rule

Any brake defect found is reported under DOC-07. For a Category 1 brake defect, apply a red tag, do not move the vehicle, and confirm the repair plan and return to service with a supervisor.

## 8. Records

Record the date, odometer reading, measured lining thickness, leak-down result and the mechanic's name on the brake inspection sheet. Keep the sheet in the vehicle file.
