# DOC-10 Lift, Jack and Lockout Safety

Document ID: DOC-10 | Version 1.0 | Owner: Fleet Maintenance and Health and Safety | Applies to: all work under or around raised vehicles

> Training document written for a portfolio project. Figures are illustrative and not official guidance.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This document covers safe lifting of vehicles and lockout before working on stored-energy systems. Working under a vehicle is one of the most dangerous jobs in the garage.

## 2. Never rely on a jack alone

1. Never get under a vehicle that is held up by a jack only. Jacks are for lifting, not for holding.
2. Support the vehicle on rated jack stands or use a vehicle hoist with its mechanical locks engaged.
3. Check the rating of the stand against the vehicle's weight, including any load.

## 3. Setting up

1. Work on firm, level ground, such as a concrete floor. Never lift on soft ground, gravel or ice.
2. Set the parking brake, put the transmission in park or neutral as appropriate, and chock the wheels that stay on the ground.
3. Use only the maker's lifting points shown in the vehicle manual.
4. Keep other people clear while lifting and lowering.

## 4. Hoists and lifts

1. Inspect the hoist before each shift for damage and leaks.
2. Never exceed the rated capacity. Do not use a hoist with a damaged lock or a leaking cylinder. Tag it out.

## 5. Lockout and tagout

Before working on a system that can store energy (air brakes, hydraulics, battery circuits, suspension), lock out and tag out:

1. Shut off the engine and remove the key. Keep the key with you.
2. Disconnect the battery negative cable if you will work on electrical circuits.
3. Bleed off stored air or hydraulic pressure and secure any raised parts (see DOC-05).
4. Attach your personal lock and tag. Only the person who put on a lock may remove it.

## 6. Wheel removal

Before wheel removal, the vehicle must be properly supported under sections 2 and 3. Break loose wheel nuts while the wheel is still on the ground.

## 7. Supervisor confirmation

For any job where you are not sure the vehicle is secure, stop and confirm with a supervisor before going under it. This is a safety-critical rule.
