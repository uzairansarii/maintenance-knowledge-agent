# DOC-09 Coolant and Cooling System Checks

Document ID: DOC-09 | Version 1.0 | Owner: Fleet Maintenance | Applies to: all diesel and gasoline vehicles

> Training document written for a portfolio project. Figures are illustrative and not official guidance.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This document explains coolant level checks, the coolant mix, hose and belt checks, and what to do if an engine overheats.

## 2. Checking the coolant level

1. Check the level only when the engine is cold. Look at the level marks on the surge tank.
2. Never open the radiator or surge tank cap on a hot engine. Hot coolant under pressure can scald you badly.
3. A coolant level that keeps dropping is a defect. Report it under DOC-07.

## 3. Coolant type and mix

1. Use the approved extended-life coolant mixed 50/50 with distilled water.
2. Never mix different coolant types or colours.
3. Test the freeze protection with a refractometer every 6 months and before winter. The mix should protect to at least -37 C.

## 4. Hoses, belts and radiator

1. Squeeze hoses when the engine is cold. Replace hoses that are cracked, bulging or soft.
2. Check belts for cracks and fraying and for correct tension.
3. Clear dirt, leaves and insects from the front of the radiator and the charge-air cooler.

## 5. Overheating response

1. If the temperature gauge enters the red zone or a warning light comes on, pull over in a safe place as soon as you can.
2. Let the engine idle for 2 to 3 minutes to cool, then shut it off. Switch on hazard lights.
3. Do not open the cap. Wait at least 30 minutes for the engine to cool.
4. Do not restart until the cause is found. Call your supervisor and report a defect under DOC-07.

## 6. Service interval

Change coolant every 3 years or 600,000 km, whichever comes first, and flush the system at the same time.

## 7. Used coolant disposal

Used coolant is hazardous waste. Put it in the labelled used-coolant drum. Never pour it onto the ground or down a drain. Clean up spills at once with absorbent.
